## Page 3 - Gradient Translation

### Title

THE ROUTE ABOVE THE GROUND

### Opening

Routing systems often begin by separating instruction from condition. A map, workflow, access sequence, or completion marker becomes the authorized layer through which the ground may be encountered.

### System equation

GROUND -> EXTERNAL DIRECTION LAYER -> COMPLIANCE -> LOSS OF GROUND

### System fields

**INPUT**  
A person asks for, receives, or is assigned a direction.

**CONTROL LAYER**  
The lived condition is abstracted into a route held elsewhere.

**RULE**  
Compliance with the indicator stands in for contact with ground.

**FAILURE**  
The routing layer becomes more authoritative than the conditions it claims merely to describe.

**MATERIAL COUNTERFACT**  
Direction cannot be separated from the load-bearing surface. Moving the sign means removing the floor.

### Limit

This translation names an institutional structure. It does not exhaust the case or convert Zen into interface theory.
